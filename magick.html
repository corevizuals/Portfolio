Magick — Sticker Rescue Tool

01
The Problem

A small grief nobody talks about.

I carry my water bottle everywhere. People hear me coming before they see me because of the clanking. Every sticker on it is intentional — a night market in Bangkok, a tiny venue before the band was famous, a shop in a city I might never go back to.

When a sticker starts to fade, there is nothing you can do. I googled how to fix it. Nothing. That absence became the entire design brief.

🔬

The science: UV radiation permanently destroys dye molecules — photodegradation. The molecule is structurally destroyed. You cannot chemically reverse it. But you can optically restore the appearance and stop further damage.

🏪

The gap: Every existing solution either prevents fading before it starts (UV sprays, laminating) or replaces the sticker entirely (reprint services). Nothing helps when fading has already begun. That gap is Magick's entire market.

💔

The emotion: The sticker from a place you cannot return to. The one from an artist who stopped selling. The gift from someone who mattered. These are not cosmetic problems — they are small griefs.

Affinity map — 4 themes

Clustered from interview quotes and observations.

Identity and meaning

  • "Stickers are intentional — each one took thought"
  • "This is basically me at this point"
  • Stickers as a visual diary of places, moments, people
  • The bottle as a personal brand object

Irreplaceability

  • "I cannot find it again" — artist stopped selling
  • Stickers from countries they are not returning to
  • Gift stickers carry the giver's emotional weight
  • A digital copy or reprint would not feel the same

Current workarounds (all fail)

  • Clear tape — "looked worse"
  • Nail polish — "turned cloudy"
  • Googling — no useful results found
  • Most assumed fading was simply inevitable

Willingness to act

  • Would pay for restoration of an irreplaceable sticker
  • Surprise that restoration was even possible
  • Pain activates for specific stickers, not all equally
  • Strong reaction to before/after demo — "satisfying"

How Might We

25 questions. One north star.

"How might we build a universal rescue tool that restores fading sticker color and preserves the memory behind it — so people never have to lose a sticker they love?"

Core: How might we help people restore a fading sticker so it looks the way it did when they first got it?
Memory: How might we preserve the story behind a sticker even after the physical sticker is gone?
Identity: How might we help people maintain the personal identity expressed through the stickers on their everyday objects?
Tech: How might we use AI to reconstruct what a sticker originally looked like from a faded or damaged version?
Community: How might we build a social platform around sticker culture that does not feel like Instagram?
Physical: How might we design a tool that anyone can use in under 2 minutes with no prior knowledge?
04
Design Principles

Six principles from the research.

Every screen, interaction, and product decision traces back to these six.

🔄

Never replace, only restore

The original sticker always stays. Magick works on top of what is there, not instead of it.

🌐

Universal, not proprietary

Works on any sticker from any source on any surface. We do not sell stickers or require our own ecosystem.

🤝

Honest about what is possible

UV damage cannot be chemically reversed. Magick restores appearance and prevents further loss — a real promise.

💾

Memory is the product

The Sticker Vault is the long-term value. Even when a sticker is gone, the reconstructed original remains.

🎁

Interaction requires generosity

To react to someone's sticker you give one of your own. Every action costs something real.

🗺️

Discovery through geography

The world map replaces the feed. You find things by curiosity and place, not by algorithm.

06
The Solution

Two stages. One product.

01

The pen — Stage 1 physical rescue

A dual-tip pen. One end re-adheres lifting edges and curled corners. The other applies a UV-blocking revival coat that optically restores remaining color and seals the sticker against future fading. Under two minutes. No app needed. Works on any sticker, any surface, from any source.

02

The app — Stage 2 AI reconstruction

Point your camera at any sticker. The app detects the boundary, corrects for surface curvature — bottle, laptop, helmet — and AI rebuilds the original colors using color deconvolution and inpainting. A before and after drag comparison. Saved permanently to your Sticker Vault.

03

The overlay — optional reprint

For severely damaged stickers, the AI reconstruction becomes a print file. A translucent adhesive film cut to exact dimensions ships to you. AR guides precise placement. The original sticker stays underneath. Its story is preserved.

04

The community — world map

Every sticker pinned to where it came from. City sticker walls, chaotic and layered. Matching with people who have the same sticker. Interactions that replace likes with something real — to react, you give one of your own stickers.

Lo-fi sketches

15+ ideas brainstormed. Decision matrix scored each on desirability, feasibility, viability, novelty. Two-stage rescue tool selected. Key pivot: rejected "magic sticker" concept in favour of a rescue tool that works on any sticker anyone already owns.

09
Key Outcomes

What the project delivered.

🔬

Validated a real, uncontested market gap

Zero existing products restore a sticker that is already fading. Every participant confirmed workarounds failed. "Why does this not exist already?" — Participant 5, unprompted.

✦

Full end-to-end UX process documented

Discovery, 5 user interviews, affinity mapping, 25 HMW questions, 6 design principles, product overview, workflow, wireframes, and 3 prototype iterations — all from scratch.

📱

Functional coded prototype — 10 screens, real device tested

React + Vite app with real routing, localStorage persistence, draggable before/after slider, interactive world map, AR-guided order flow. Tested on iPhone via local network.

🌍

Anti-Instagram community model

Replaced likes, comments, and follows with "slap it on," geography-first discovery, and a matching system. Every interaction costs something real — generosity is built into the mechanic.

10
Lessons Learned

What I would do differently.

01

The pivot is the product

Moving from "magic sticker" to "rescue tool" changed everything. The reframe happened because of research — not instinct. That is what user research is actually for.

02

Test earlier, test scrappier

The think-aloud session with Jade revealed the scan screen confusion in 10 minutes. A paper prototype would have found it even faster. Earlier testing means more time to fix.

03

Personal experience is valid data

The clanking bottle, the sticker from a market I might not return to — that personal origin story was not anecdote. It was the clearest articulation of the user problem.
