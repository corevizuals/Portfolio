The Challenge

The problem that needed solving.

Groupe MMI's website had an outdated design, unclear hierarchy, and poor content organization. Users struggled to navigate, leading to high bounce rates, while internal teams faced difficulties updating content in the CMS. The business needed a polished, modern website that strengthened credibility and improved user engagement.

🎨

Outdated layout with unclear visual hierarchy and inconsistent styling across pages.

📉

Poor content organization was causing users to drop off early — high bounce rates across key pages.

⚙️

The CMS was difficult for internal teams to manage, creating delays on every content update.

🏢

The business needed a more credible, polished online presence to support client acquisition and trust.

02
Process & Solution

How it came together.

01

UX Audit

Conducted a thorough UX audit identifying pain points, content gaps, and usability issues across the full site.

02

Redesign

Rebuilt core page templates — homepage, services, about, contact — with improved hierarchy, readability, and visual consistency.

03

Navigation

Restructured the navigation to reduce friction and create clearer, more intuitive user paths to key information.

04

Content Strategy

Optimized content blocks for clarity, SEO, scannability, and conversion — rewriting and reorganizing key pages.

05

Testing & Launch

Validated redesign decisions through moderated user testing, then implemented in WordPress with CMS documentation for the internal team.

Lessons Learned

What this project taught me.

01

A UX audit is the most valuable first step

Going into a redesign without understanding why the existing site was failing would have produced a prettier version of the same problems.

02

CMS design is UX too

Making the backend easier for the internal team to manage had as much long-term impact as the visual redesign.

03

User testing before launch prevents expensive rework

Two rounds of moderated testing caught navigation assumptions that wouldn't have been obvious from the design alone.
