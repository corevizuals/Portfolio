The Challenge

The problem that needed solving.

Improved the online shopping experience for Kérastase by designing and testing pictograms that highlight product benefits and fragrances. Increased product page visits, clarified information for users, and optimized layouts for both desktop and mobile, while ensuring all designs met brand approval requirements.

🔍

Users struggled to quickly understand product benefits and collections from product tiles alone.

📉

PDP access rates from CLPs were low: 64.5% Kérastase

✅

All designs required approval from the DMI (L'Oréal Groupe brand manager) before launch.

📱

Mobile implementation was especially challenging — pictogram images took excessive space relative to product titles.

02
Process & Solution

How it came together.

01

Research

Conducted user research and testing sessions to identify pain points in how product benefits were communicated on CLPs.

02

Design

Ideated and designed multiple pictogram/icon systems in Figma to visually communicate product benefits directly on tiles.

03

Build

Implemented A/B test variants using JavaScript within A/B Tasty's editor, dynamically injecting pictograms into product tile DOM elements.

04

Test

Ran A/B tests across Kérastase CLPs, monitoring CTR, add-to-cart rate, revenue per visitor, and AOV.

05

Iterate

Collaborated with Kérastase team, DMI, and stakeholders for feedback rounds and approvals before each variant launch.

Tools & Stack

Fragrance Notes — Second Workstream

Taking pictograms from benefits to scent.

On top of the A/B testing work, I was tasked with extending the pictogram approach to Kérastase's olfactory identity — designing a fragrance notes system for product pages and implementing it as a dedicated tab under each product.

Kérastase's Gloss Absolu signature scent is built on a structured olfactory pyramid — an haute-couture blend of citrus top notes, a luxury floral bouquet heart, and warm base notes. My task was to translate that invisible sensory experience into a visual pictogram system that could live on the product page alongside the hair benefit pictograms.

The Olfactory Pyramid — Gloss Absolu

🌤 Top Notes

Bergamot · Lemon · Cardamom

Sparkling citrus opening — the first sensory impression

🌸 Heart Notes

Rose · Jasmine · Freesia · Iris · Orange Blossom · Green Tea

Balanced, feminine floral bouquet — the signature depth

🪵 Base Notes

Vanilla · Sandalwood

Warm, lingering close — the memory of the scent

01

Pictogram Design for Fragrance Notes

Designed a pictogram system representing each fragrance note in the olfactory pyramid — translating abstract scent descriptors (bergamot, iris, sandalwood) into visual icons that communicated both the note itself and its position in the fragrance story.

02

Fragrance Tab — Product Detail Pages

Implemented the fragrance notes as a dedicated "Fragrance" tab at the bottom of every Kérastase product page — sitting alongside the existing hair benefit pictograms. The tab showcases the full olfactory pyramid with top, heart, and base note pictograms, giving users a sensory preview of the product before purchase.

03

Hair Benefit Pictograms on PDPs

In parallel, the hair benefit pictograms tested in the A/B experiments were also implemented directly on the product detail pages — making the icon system consistent across both CLPs and PDPs, reinforcing comprehension throughout the full purchase journey.

05
Lessons Learned

What this project taught me.

01

Test everything, assume nothing

A/B testing proved that even small visual additions like pictograms had measurable revenue impact — the data validated what intuition couldn't guarantee.

02

Design systems scale

The hair benefit pictogram system established a visual language that transferred directly to the fragrance notes project — proving that well-designed systems compound in value across contexts.

03

Sensory → visual is a real design challenge

Translating abstract scent descriptors into pictograms required a different design instinct than benefit icons — scent is emotional and invisible, so every icon had to earn its association.

04

Stakeholder alignment is design work

Getting DMI approval required presenting data-backed rationale at every stage. Cross-functional alignment — across UX, brand, and global teams — is as much a design skill as Figma.
