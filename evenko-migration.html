The Challenge

The problem that needed solving.

Montréal en Lumières needed 1000+ events, artist pages, and venues spanning 20+ years migrated without data loss or broken links — under tight deadlines, a reduced team, and complex coordination across IT, design, marketing, and event teams.

📂

Thousands of events with unique content structures, media assets, and schedule complexities — each requiring individual validation.

👥

Team of 5 reduced to 2 active members due to illness, maternity leave, and vacation during the critical window.

⏱️

Tight deadline with no buffer — any data loss or broken links would surface immediately to a live public audience.

🔗

Coordinating approvals across IT, design, marketing, and event management teams under compressed timelines.

02
Process & Solution

How it came together.

01

Content Audit

Conducted a full audit of existing content and media — identifying duplicates, outdated assets, and formatting inconsistencies across all 1000+ events.

02

Migration Planning

Developed a phased migration plan minimizing downtime risk while maintaining UX consistency across web and app platforms.

03

Execution

Automated repetitive data mapping tasks with scripts; manually validated complex content requiring human review.

04

QA

Ran QA checklists against event details, artist schedules, venue maps, linked media, and navigation for every migrated record.

05

Post-Launch Monitoring

Monitored the live platform in real time post-migration, resolving issues immediately to preserve user engagement.

Lessons Learned

What this project taught me.

01

Checklists are force multipliers

With only 2 people handling 1000+ records, a rigorous QA checklist was the only way to maintain quality at that scale.

02

Automation earns its cost upfront

Time spent scripting repetitive tasks paid back immediately — manual validation of edge cases was where human judgment was actually needed.

03

Constraints reveal your real priorities

Running a critical migration with half the team forced ruthless prioritization — a skill that transfers directly to any production environment.
