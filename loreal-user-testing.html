UX Research & Card Sorting Workshops

Led interactive card-sorting workshops in Figma to collect quantitative and qualitative user data for Kiehl's and La Roche-Posay. The goal was to validate whether menu and sub-menu categories were organized intuitively, identify navigation pain points, and inform a full menu architecture redesign.

The Challenge

The problem that needed solving.

Kiehl's and La Roche-Posay noticed that users struggled to find products on their websites. Menu categories were unclear, click-through rates were low, and it wasn’t obvious if the structure matched how customers naturally think about the products. The goal was to understand these issues and create a more intuitive navigation system.

📊

Both brands had noticed low click-through rates in their menu bars — the hypothesis was that users couldn't find relevant categories.

🗂️

Needed to validate whether existing menu and sub-menu taxonomy matched how real users actually think about products.

👥

Recruiting participants required careful outreach — used an existing volunteer pool via email blast with brand gifts as incentives.

🔬

First time leading a full user research study and writing the test script with a senior UX researcher.

02
Process & Solution

How it came together.

01

Script Writing

Wrote the questionnaire and facilitation script in collaboration with a senior UX researcher, learning which question formats yield reliable behavioral data.

02

Workshop Design

Designed the card-sorting exercise in Figma — creating digital post-its for sub-menu titles and categories (e.g. "SPF 30+" to be placed under "Sunscreen").

03

Participant Recruitment

Recruited from an existing volunteer pool via targeted email blast, offering L'Oréal brand gifts as participation incentives.

04

Facilitation

Conducted moderated sessions, guiding participants through the sorting exercise while observing and noting behavioral patterns.

05

Analysis

Systematically analyzed placement patterns across all participants to identify consistent taxonomy structures and outliers.

Lessons Learned

What this project taught me.

01

Questions shape answers

The framing of workshop questions determines the quality of insights. Learned why certain formats work — and which ones introduce bias.

02

Observation is data

Watching where participants hesitated, second-guessed, or laughed revealed as much as where they placed the cards.

03

Interactive research yields richer data

A card-sorting exercise produces both quantitative patterns and qualitative nuance in a single session.
